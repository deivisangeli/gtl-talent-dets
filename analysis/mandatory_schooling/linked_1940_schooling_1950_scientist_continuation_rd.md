# Continuation-school cohort cutoffs

Run `Rscript analysis/mandatory_schooling/estimate_linked_1940_schooling_1950_scientist_continuation_rd.R`
from the repository root. Run the companion `test_...continuation_rd.R` to check the
event screen and stacked estimator. Outputs are written under Dropbox
`results/mandatory_schooling/linked_1940_schooling_1950_scientist_continuation_rd/`.

The analysis uses the exact main linked 1940-schooling/1950-occupation IV
population, aggregated in `main_cells.csv`. Birth cohort is `1940 - AGE - 1`.
Each reform cutoff is the first birth cohort with a positive change in required
schooling years relative to retaining the preceding statute until its next
change. Three annual cohorts must have zero reform increment before the cutoff,
and three must have a positive increment after it. The existing reform audit
also excludes overlapping positive cutoffs and first-law cutoffs.

There are 29 state introductions of continuation-school provisions by 1930.
The law audit finds 17 clean continuation-field reforms in 16 states: 14 first
introductions and three later revisions. Four change only continuation fields:
Delaware 1921, Iowa 1919, New Jersey 1919, and Ohio 1922. The other 13 also
change regular attendance, work-permit, or early-exit rules. Of the 17, the
continuation fields themselves raise required years at the cutoff in 15. The
pooled 17-event estimate is therefore a **full reform-package** effect, not
the isolated effect of continuation schooling.

The stacked local linear model uses event intercepts, event-specific slopes on
each side, triangular distance weights multiplied by IV-cell counts, and
birth-state-clustered standard errors. The indicator for cohorts at or after
the cutoff instruments completed schooling in the fuzzy specification. The
four-state continuation-only contrasts are descriptive, without a fuzzy ratio
or cluster-based interval. Adjusted and raw discontinuity plots and a
four-state raw plot are generated as PDF and PNG.
`continuation_reforms_by_state.png` additionally shows raw person-weighted
means and separate descriptive fits for all three outcomes at each of the 17
cutoffs. Ohio's two reforms appear as distinct cards; each card identifies a
continuation-only or bundled change. The legal outcome is the incremental
requirement generated by the full reform.

In the 17-event sample (1,485,537 linked men; 2,889 scientists), the pooled
required-years jump is 0.824 and the schooling jump is 0.032 years (SE 0.018).
The scientist reduced-form jump is 14.7 per 100,000 (SE 9.9). The fuzzy ratio
is 464.6 per 100,000 per schooling year, but its first stage is weak. Within
the Anderson--Rubin search grid of -5,000 to +5,000 per 100,000, accepted
values occupy [-5,000, -2,379] and [-172, 5,000], reaching both grid edges.
The fixed-bandwidth Calonico calculation is a diagnostic only: the running
variable has just three distinct birth-cohort values on each side. Its robust
interval is also wide.

The four continuation-only events include 347,401 men and 861 scientists.
Their pooled descriptive schooling contrast is +0.043 years and scientist
contrast is -4.2 per 100,000. Four independent states do not support reliable
clustered inference. The linked sample is selected by successful cross-census
matching, and completed-age birth cohorts are measured with error near the
cutoff.
